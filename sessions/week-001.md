# Week 001: Average Speed — Distance Divided by Time

> **Track:** Physics and aerospace · **Level:** National 5 beginner · **Time:** 60 minutes

## Why This Matters

Average speed answers a useful question: **how much distance was covered per unit of time?** You can use it to compare journeys, estimate travel time, analyse a race, or check the motion of a robot. It is also a first step towards motion graphs and aerospace calculations.

## Prerequisites

You should be able to divide positive numbers and recognise common units such as metres (m), seconds (s), kilometres (km), minutes (min), and hours (h). No earlier session is required.

**Materials:** pencil, paper, and a calculator. A ruler is optional for making a neat evidence table.

## Simple Explanation — 15 Minutes

### The one central idea

**Average speed is total distance divided by total elapsed time.** This relationship is confirmed by both OpenStax and The Physics Classroom.[1][2]

\[
\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total elapsed time}}
\]

A useful symbol form is:

\[
v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{d}{t}
\]

Think of it as sharing the whole distance equally across every unit of time. If a journey's average speed is 4 m/s, the journey covered an average of 4 metres during each second.

### Four important terms

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Distance** | The total length of the route travelled. |
| **Elapsed time** | The total time from start to finish, including stops. |
| **Average speed** | Total distance divided by total elapsed time. |
| **Rate** | A comparison of how much one quantity changes per unit of another. |

### Units must match

Distance divided by time creates a “distance per time” unit. The standard scientific unit of speed is metres per second (m/s), while kilometres per hour (km/h) is also commonly used.[1]

| Distance used | Time used | Speed unit |
|---|---|---|
| metres | seconds | m/s |
| kilometres | hours | km/h |

Do not divide kilometres by minutes and label the result km/h. Convert the minutes to hours first:

\[
\text{hours} = \frac{\text{minutes}}{60}
\]

### What “average” does not mean

An object can speed up, slow down, or stop during a journey. Average speed uses the **whole** distance and the **whole** elapsed time; it does not claim that the object moved at one steady speed throughout.[1]

Use this reliable method every time:

1. Write down the **total distance**.
2. Write down the **total elapsed time**.
3. Convert units if needed.
4. Divide distance by time.
5. Write the speed unit and check whether the size makes sense.

## Worked Examples — 15 Minutes

### Example 1: metres and seconds already match

A runner covers 120 m in 30 s. Find the average speed.

1. Write the relationship: \(v_{\text{avg}} = d/t\).
2. Substitute the values: \(v_{\text{avg}} = 120\text{ m}/30\text{ s}\).
3. Divide: \(120 \div 30 = 4\).
4. Combine the units: metres per second.

\[
\boxed{v_{\text{avg}} = 4\text{ m/s}}
\]

**Sense check:** \(4\text{ m/s} \times 30\text{ s} = 120\text{ m}\), so the answer returns the original distance.

### Example 2: convert minutes to hours

A bicycle travels 2.4 km in 12 min. Find the average speed in km/h.

1. Convert the time: \(12 \div 60 = 0.2\text{ h}\).
2. Substitute: \(v_{\text{avg}} = 2.4\text{ km}/0.2\text{ h}\).
3. Divide: \(2.4 \div 0.2 = 12\).

\[
\boxed{v_{\text{avg}} = 12\text{ km/h}}
\]

**Common mistake:** \(2.4 \div 12 = 0.2\) gives km/min, not km/h.

### Example 3: a stop counts in elapsed time

A person walks 1.2 km from 14:00 to 14:30 and rests briefly on the way. Find the average speed in km/h.

1. Total distance: \(1.2\text{ km}\).
2. Total elapsed time: \(30\text{ min} = 30/60 = 0.5\text{ h}\). The rest is included because the clock kept running.
3. Divide: \(1.2 \div 0.5 = 2.4\).

\[
\boxed{v_{\text{avg}} = 2.4\text{ km/h}}
\]

## Practice Activity — 15 Minutes

### Journey analyser

Create one table on paper with these columns:

| Journey | Total distance | Total elapsed time | Conversion needed | Working | Average speed | Check |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---|

Complete the four journeys below. Show every unit conversion and calculation.

1. **A:** Lewis cycles 600 m in 150 s. Find the average speed in m/s.
2. **B:** A robot simulation travels 1.8 km in 9 min. Find the average speed in km/h.
3. **C:** A test cart travels 800 m in 120 s, pauses, then travels another 400 m in 80 s. The two times include the pause. Find the average speed for the whole test in m/s.
4. **D — Challenge:** A ferry simulation travels 5 km in 24 min. Find the average speed in km/h.

For each answer, perform this check:

\[
\text{average speed} \times \text{total elapsed time} = \text{total distance}
\]

**Evidence to keep:** your completed four-row table, including units, conversions, working, and checks. Score one point for each correct speed and one point for each correct check, for a total of 8 points. Aim for at least 6/8 before moving on.

## Quiz — 5 Minutes

Answer without looking back if you can.

1. Write the average-speed relationship in words.
2. What speed unit results from dividing metres by seconds?
3. A drone simulation covers 450 m in 90 s. What is its average speed?
4. A train model covers 3 km in 15 min. What is its average speed in km/h?
5. Why must a stop be included when finding average speed for the full journey?

## Answers

### Practice activity

1. **A:** \(600/150 = \boxed{4\text{ m/s}}\). Check: \(4 \times 150 = 600\text{ m}\).
2. **B:** \(9\text{ min} = 9/60 = 0.15\text{ h}\); \(1.8/0.15 = \boxed{12\text{ km/h}}\). Check: \(12 \times 0.15 = 1.8\text{ km}\).
3. **C:** total distance \(= 800 + 400 = 1200\text{ m}\); total time \(= 120 + 80 = 200\text{ s}\); \(1200/200 = \boxed{6\text{ m/s}}\). Check: \(6 \times 200 = 1200\text{ m}\).
4. **D:** \(24\text{ min} = 24/60 = 0.4\text{ h}\); \(5/0.4 = \boxed{12.5\text{ km/h}}\). Check: \(12.5 \times 0.4 = 5\text{ km}\).

### Quiz

1. Average speed equals total distance divided by total elapsed time.
2. Metres per second, written **m/s**.
3. \(450/90 = \boxed{5\text{ m/s}}\).
4. \(15\text{ min} = 0.25\text{ h}\), so \(3/0.25 = \boxed{12\text{ km/h}}\).
5. Average speed uses the full elapsed time from start to finish. During a stop, time continues even though no distance is added, so leaving it out would make the calculated speed too high.
